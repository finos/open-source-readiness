Financial services organisations operate in a unique environment. Regulatory obligations, security requirements, operational resilience expectations, complex organisational structures, and legacy technology landscapes can all influence how InnerSource is introduced and scaled. The experiences and guidance captured in this playbook reflect the realities of applying InnerSource within these environments.

Whether you are exploring InnerSource for the first time, launching an initial pilot, scaling existing practices, or evaluating how emerging technologies such as AI can support collaboration and reuse, different sections of this playbook have been designed to help at each stage of the journey.

## How to Use This Playbook

The sections below follow a typical InnerSource journey: picking a first project and setting it up well, making the case for continued investment, putting clear ownership and compliance guardrails in place as more teams get involved, and preparing for the way AI coding agents are changing how software is built. You can read them in order, or go straight to the stage that matches where your organisation is today.

### If You're New to InnerSource

Most journeys begin with a single pilot. Choosing the right project and setting it up properly gives the initiative an early, visible success to build on.

Start with:
- [Identifying InnerSource Project Candidates](./Best-Candidates.md) to understand what makes a good InnerSource candidate and how to choose an effective pilot
- [InnerSource Blueprint](./Blueprint.md) to learn how to establish an InnerSource-ready project, including documentation, ownership, governance, contribution workflows, discoverability, and community engagement

### If You Need to Make the Case for Investment

Once a pilot is running, sponsors will want to know what it is worth. Showing value in financial terms is often what keeps an InnerSource initiative funded beyond its first year.

Read:
- [InnerSource ROI Model](./ROI-Model.md) to understand the four key value areas of InnerSource (development cost savings, reduced cost of ownership, time-to-market gains, and contributions from other teams) and how to measure them in dollar terms for reporting to leadership

### If You're Scaling Beyond the First Projects

As more teams start to contribute to and depend on shared projects, expectations need to be explicit. In a regulated organisation, collaboration also has to respect legal entity and regulatory boundaries.

Read:
- [InnerSource Ownership Models](./Ownership-Models.md) to decide who maintains a project, what the maintainers commit to, and what contributions they are open to receiving, so that contributors, consumers, and leaders all know what to expect
- [Legal & Compliance](./Legal-and-Compliance/Introduction.md) to understand how regulatory requirements, such as [UK Ring-Fencing](./Legal-and-Compliance/UK%20Ring%20Fencing.md), can affect how InnerSource collaboration is structured, and where ownership, support, and accountability should sit

### If You're Adopting AI Coding Agents

AI coding agents make code cheap to produce, and that includes duplicated code. This raises the stakes for reuse and governance across the organisation.

Read:
- [Agentic Development with InnerSource](./Agentic-Development.md) to learn why InnerSource matters more, not less, when agents are writing code, how shared, well-governed projects make agent output cheaper and safer, and how agent tooling removes some of InnerSource's traditional adoption barriers
